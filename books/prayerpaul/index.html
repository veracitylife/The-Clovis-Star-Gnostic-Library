Pearson writes, &quot;This short prayer, inscribed on both sides of the front flyleaf of the codex, consists of three parts. In the first part (A, 1-14) the petitioner addresses the preexistent Father, through Jesus Christ, and prays for his return to the place whence he came. In the second part (A, 15-25) he prays for bodily health and spiritual illumination, and invokes the authority of &#39;the evangelist,&#39; presumably Paul, the &#39;preacher of the Gospel.&#39; In the third part (A,25-B,2) he prays for what no angelic eye has seen, no ruler ears have heard, and what has not entered the human heart (compare 1 Corinthians 2:9).

The prayer concludes with a doxology (B,2-5). The title that is appended to the tractate, &#39;prayer of Paul the apostle,&#39; is given in Greek, the original language of the prayer as a whole.&quot; (Ancient Gnosticism, p. 173) On the allusions to phrases found in other literature, Madaleine Scopello comments, &quot;The second part of the Prayer of the Apostle Paul invokes the divine as &#39;you who exist and preexisted.&#39; These titles, with a philosophical flavor, appear quite often in Valentinian as well as Sethian Gnostic literature in reference to the highest God. The formula &#39;the name exalted above every name&#39; derives from Philippians 2:9, as Dieter Mueller notes, the author of the Prayer shows a clear knowledge of the Psalms and the Pauline epistles.

We concur with this line of interpretation, especially concerning the five titles given to Jesus Christ: Lord of lords, King of eternal realms (or aeons, ages), Son of Humanity, Spirit, Advocate (or Paraclete) of truth. The title &#39;Lord of lords&#39; is also present in 1 Timothy 6:15 and Revelation 17:14; 19:16, each time in connection with the title &#39;King of kings,&#39; &#39;King of the ages&#39; appears as a title in Tobit 13:6-10, 1 Timothy 1:17, and Revelation 15:3. Although &#39;Son of Humanity&#39; is very frequent in the New Testament and early Christian literature, &#39;Advocate of truth&#39; seems to come from John 15:26 (cf. also, for &#39;Paraclete,&#39; John 14:26; 16:7; 1 John 2:1).&quot; (The Nag Hammadi Scriptures, p.
